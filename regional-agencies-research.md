#### Case ST-1: ST3 Program Cost Explosion ($22–34.5B Shortfall)

**Date surfaced:** August 28, 2025 (initial announcement); March 21, 2026 (updated $34.5B figure per board retreat)

**What happened:**
Sound Transit released "bottom-up" capital cost re-estimates for ST3 megaprojects in August 2025, revealing a $22–30B shortfall in 2025 dollars versus the 2016 voter-approved finance plan. Specific project overruns:
- West Seattle Link: grew from $4.2B (finance plan) to $7.0–7.9B
- Ballard Link: grew from ~$11.9B (finance plan) to $20.1–22.6B

By March 2026, at a board retreat, agency staff revised the total program shortfall to approximately $34.5B. The agency announced a formal "program reset" process to determine which projects to build, delay, or descope within existing taxing authority. No new voter-approved funding increase had been sought.

**Dollar amount:** $22–34.5B estimated capital shortfall (program-level, not a specific misappropriation)

**Source tier and URLs:**
- Tier 2 (Seattle Times): https://www.seattletimes.com/seattle-news/transportation/sound-transits-expansion-plans-balloon-by-up-to-30-billion/ (Aug 28, 2025)
- Tier 2 (The Urbanist): https://www.theurbanist.org/sound-transit-plans-major-program-reset-in-face-of-30-to-40-billion-shortfall/ (Aug 28, 2025)
- Tier 2 (Lynnwood Times): https://lynnwoodtimes.com/2026/03/21/sound-transit-budget/ (Mar 21, 2026)

**Key actors named in media:** Terri Mestas (Deputy CEO, Megaproject Delivery), Victoria Wass (Deputy CEO for Finance), Dow Constantine (CEO)

**Classification note:** Two independent Tier 2 sources on the August disclosure; additional Tier 2 coverage on the March 2026 update. Meets the Tier 2×2 bar. This is distinct from the earlier West Seattle Link entry in any existing registry — this is the program-wide cost explosion and board-directed reset.

#### Case ST-2: CEO Selection — Conflict of Interest / Closed-Door Process (Constantine)

**Date surfaced:** February 2025 (Seattle Times/The Urbanist revealed Constantine was a candidate); March 28, 2025 (appointment); March 26, 2025 (ethics complaint filed); June 18, 2025 (dismissed)

**What happened:**
Board member and then-King County Executive Dow Constantine was selected as Sound Transit CEO through a process that transit advocates and one complainant alleged was a closed-door conflict of interest. Constantine had appointed 9 of 18 Sound Transit board members in his capacity as King County Executive, raising questions about his ability to be evaluated impartially by a board he largely appointed.

On March 26, 2025, Jennifer Aspelund filed Ethics Complaint OMB-2025-0160 with the King County Office of the Ombuds, alleging violation of the King County Ethics Code regarding conflict of interest in the CEO selection process.

On June 18, 2025, the Ombuds dismissed the complaint, finding no Ethics Code violation. The Ombuds found Constantine did not participate in his own evaluation as a county executive (he had been a board member in a separate capacity), and that the hiring process did not constitute a conflict of interest under the applicable code provisions.

**Dollar amount:** N/A (governance/process complaint)

**Source tier and URLs:**
- Tier 1 (King County Ombuds Report OMB-2025-0160, June 18, 2025 dismissal): https://cdn.kingcounty.gov/-/media/king-county/independent/governance-and-leadership/government-oversight/office-of-the-ombuds/reports/omb-2025-0160_ethics_report.pdf
- Tier 2 (The Urbanist, appointment coverage): https://www.theurbanist.org/sound-transit-hires-constantine-as-ceo/ (Mar 28, 2025)
- Tier 2 (The Urbanist, conflict allegations): https://www.theurbanist.org/constantine-pushes-back-on-conflict-allegations-surrounding-sound-transit-ceo-bid/ (Feb 26, 2025)

**Key actors named in media:** Dow Constantine (CEO, former King County Executive, former ST Board member), Jennifer Aspelund (complainant), David Scott (Seattle Subway, publicly critical of process)

**Outcome:** Dismissed — no Ethics Code violation found.

**Classification note:** Tier 1 source exists (official Ombuds report). Meets bar. Outcome was dismissal, which should be noted prominently if ported to the case registry.

#### Case ST-3: Terri Mestas Salary — Above-Supervisor Compensation at Hire

**Date surfaced:** March 29, 2024

**What happened:**
Sound Transit hired Terri Mestas as Deputy CEO for Megaproject Delivery at $600,000/year — a salary exceeding that of her direct supervisor, then-Interim CEO Goran Sparrman ($375,000/year). The advertised salary range for the position was $350,000–$550,000; Mestas was hired above the top of the posted range. Mestas came from LAX (Los Angeles World Airports). The board was briefed on the hire; no formal complaint or ethics referral was filed, but the anomaly was publicly surfaced and reported by the Seattle Times.

**Dollar amount:** $600,000/year (Mestas) vs. $375,000/year (Sparrman, then-supervisor)

**Source tier and URLs:**
- Tier 2 (Seattle Times): https://www.seattletimes.com/seattle-news/transportation/sound-transit-will-pay-600k-to-new-megaproject-leader-hired-from-lax/ (Mar 29, 2024)

**Key actors named in media:** Terri Mestas (Deputy CEO, Megaproject Delivery), Goran Sparrman (Interim CEO), Dow Constantine (board chair at time)

**Classification note:** Single Tier 2 source (Seattle Times) — does not fully meet the Tier 2×2 bar, and no Tier 1 source found. Flagged as a borderline case. Include as informational; annotate as single-source. Could be strengthened if public records show board compensation approval documents.

#### Case POS-1: Partner in Employment (PIE) Contractor Fraud — $250K Misappropriation, Inflated Reporting

**Date surfaced:** Fraud discovered May 2024; Port notified June 19, 2024; Internal Audit Report #2024-17 released December 2024

**What happened:**
Partner in Employment (PIE), a nonprofit serving refugee and immigrant job-seekers, held three Port of Seattle workforce development contracts totaling approximately $509,000. In May 2024, a PIE staff member misappropriated $250,000 from the organization (the specific mechanism is not disclosed in the audit report). PIE's Executive Director, Hien Kieu, confirmed the misappropriation to Port staff. PIE reported the theft to the King County Sheriff; a criminal investigation was opened as of December 2024.

Port Internal Audit Report #2024-17 found that PIE's performance reports to the Port were "materially inflated":
- April 2024: PIE reported 45 clients served; actual was 10
- April 2024: PIE reported 21 job placements; actual was 0

The internal audit also found that the Port lacked formal verification procedures for contractor-reported performance metrics — both findings rated "High" severity.

**Dollar amount:** $250,000 misappropriated from PIE; ~$509,000 in Port contracts at risk; PIE's April billing was for zero verified placements.

**Source tier and URLs:**
- Tier 1 (Port of Seattle Internal Audit Report #2024-17, December 2024): https://www.portseattle.org/sites/default/files/2024-12/Partner%20in%20Employment-Audit%20Report%20No.%202024-17.pdf

**Key actors named in audit:** Hien Kieu (PIE Executive Director), Bookda Gheisar (Port Sr. Director, Office of Equity, Diversity & Inclusion — contract owner), Anna Pavlik (Port Director of Workforce Development)

**Status as of research close:** Criminal investigation open with King County Sheriff. Port implementing corrective action on performance verification controls.

#### Case POS-2: Rhysida Ransomware Attack — 90,000 Data Breach, Operational Disruption at SeaTac

**Date surfaced:** August 24, 2024 (attack); September 2024 (public disclosure of attack); April 2025 (breach notification to ~90,000 affected individuals)

**What happened:**
The Rhysida ransomware gang attacked Port of Seattle computer systems on August 24, 2024. The attack disrupted SeaTac Airport operations for several days, affecting baggage systems, check-in kiosks, public Wi-Fi, and flight information display boards. The Port refused to pay the demanded $6 million ransom. Rhysida subsequently published some stolen data on the dark web.

In April 2025, the Port notified approximately 90,000 individuals that their personal data was compromised in the attack. Affected data included Social Security numbers, dates of birth, driver's license numbers, and in some cases medical information. The affected population was primarily current and former Port employees, contractors, and individuals with parking records.

**Dollar amount:** $6M ransom demanded (refused); remediation costs not publicly itemized; 90,000 individuals notified.

**Source tier and URLs:**
- Tier 1 (Port of Seattle official breach notice): https://www.portseattle.org/news/port-seattle-providing-notice-individuals-affected-fall-2024-cyberattack
- Tier 2 (KOMO News): https://komonews.com/news/local/seattle-cyberattack-exposes-data-of-90000-people-raises-security-concerns
- Tier 2 (The Record, cybersecurity outlet): https://therecord.media/port-of-seattle-says-90000-impacted-in-2024-ransomware-attack

**Key actors named in media:** Steve Metruck (Port Executive Director)

**Classification note:** Tier 1 (official Port breach notice) plus two Tier 2 sources. Meets bar. Note: a prior SAO finding (2021-001) on EFT phishing internal controls was fully corrected per the April 2024 SAO report — the ransomware attack is a separate, subsequent event.

#### Case SPS-1: SAO Finding 2024-001 — Paid Lunch Equity Tool, Federal Award Compliance

**Date surfaced:** May 22, 2025 (SAO Report #1037358 published)

**What happened:**
SAO's financial statement and single audit of Seattle Public Schools for the year ended August 31, 2024 (Report #1037358, published May 22, 2025) issued Finding 2024-001, designated a "significant deficiency" in internal controls over federal awards. The finding relates to the National School Lunch Program's Paid Lunch Equity (PLE) tool, a required federal calculation that determines whether a district must raise paid lunch prices or contribute nonfederal funds to the program.

The District completed only one of two required tabs in the PLE tool. Had both tabs been completed as required, the tool would have indicated the District needed to raise lunch prices by $0.23 per meal or contribute $3,128,043 in nonfederal funds. The District was ultimately able to demonstrate compliance during the audit (avoiding a material noncompliance determination), but the SAO found inadequate internal controls for completing the tool correctly.

**Dollar amount:** $3,128,043 in potential nonfederal fund contribution requirement if tool had been fully executed; actual compliance demonstrated during audit.

**Source tier and URLs:**
- Tier 1 (SAO Report #1037358, published May 22, 2025): https://portal.sao.wa.gov/ReportSearch/Home/ViewReportFile?arn=1037358&isFinding=false&sp=false
- Tier 1 (SPS-posted copy): https://www.seattleschools.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Washington_State_Auditors_Office_Financial_Statement_and_Single_Audit_Report_May_2025.pdf

**Key actors:** Not specifically named in finding. District management acknowledged finding and provided corrective action plan.

#### Case SPS-2: Unresolved COVID Emergency Connectivity Fund Finding — $4.9M Questioned Costs, Persistent Non-Correction

**Date surfaced:** Originally issued in 2021-22 audit; persisted as "Not Corrected" through the 2023-24 audit (Report #1037358, May 22, 2025)

**What happened:**
SAO issued a material weakness and adverse opinion on the Emergency Connectivity Fund (ECF) federal program in SPS's 2021-22 audit, questioning $4,912,945 in ECF expenditures (laptops purchased under the program). The District has disputed the finding across three consecutive audit cycles. As of the 2023-24 audit (published May 2025), the finding remains marked "Not Corrected" in the Prior Audit Findings summary.

SAO's adverse opinion on an individual federal program is a serious audit outcome — it means the auditor cannot conclude the District properly administered those federal funds. The multi-year non-correction is unusual and indicates a sustained dispute between the District and SAO over $4.9M in federal program expenditures.

**Dollar amount:** $4,912,945 in questioned ECF federal funds

**Source tier and URLs:**
- Tier 1 (SAO Report #1037358, Summary of Prior Audit Findings — "Not Corrected" status confirmed May 2025): https://portal.sao.wa.gov/ReportSearch/Home/ViewReportFile?arn=1037358&isFinding=false&sp=false

**Key actors:** District management (disputed finding). SAO. No individuals named.

**Classification note:** The 2023-24 audit report (published May 2025, within window) confirms this finding remains unresolved. The active non-correction status as of the most recent audit is the qualifying event within the research window.

#### Case SPS-3: $100M+ Structural Budget Deficit — Interfund Loan Without Finance/Audit Committee

**Date surfaced:** 2024 (interfund loan authorization); deficit projections reported throughout 2024-2025 school year; Seattle Times coverage May 27, 2025

**What happened:**
Seattle Public Schools has carried a structural budget deficit requiring extraordinary measures across multiple years:

- FY 2023-24: District drew down approximately $42 million from its "Rainy Day" reserve fund to close the year's gap.
- FY 2024-25: District authorized a $27.5 million interfund loan, borrowing from its own capital fund to cover operating expenses. Critically, this loan was authorized without a Finance/Audit Committee in place — the board had dissolved the committee prior to this action.
- FY 2025-26: District repeatedly projected a structural deficit of $94–104 million. The district presented a nominally "balanced" budget proposal in May 2025 through cuts and one-time measures, but the structural gap persisted.

The dissolved Finance/Audit Committee is a governance concern: interfund loans of this scale are typically reviewed by such committees before board approval.

**Dollar amount:** $27.5M interfund loan; $42M reserve drawdown (2023-24); $94–104M structural deficit projected for 2025-26

**Source tier and URLs:**
- Tier 2 (Seattle Times, May 27, 2025): https://www.seattletimes.com/seattle-news/seattle-public-schools-presents-balanced-budget-for-now/
- Tier 1 (SPS Board Budget Study Session documents, Jan 22, 2025 — confirms interfund loan details): https://www.seattleschools.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/2025-01-22-Budget-Study-Session-FINAL-ADA.pdf

**Key actors named in media:** Brent Jones (Superintendent, announced resignation March 2025), Liza Rankin (Board President)

**Classification note:** Meets bar — Tier 1 official board document plus Tier 2 (Seattle Times). The governance angle (no Finance/Audit Committee when the interfund loan was authorized) elevates this above a pure policy/budget disagreement.

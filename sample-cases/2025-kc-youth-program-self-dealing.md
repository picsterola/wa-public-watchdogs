## What happened

A King County program manager overseeing an anti-racism youth initiative is alleged, per an internal county investigation summarized in local television reporting, to have steered approximately $813,000 in grant funds to four relatives — her daughter, two brothers, a cousin, and a sister-in-law — through subcontracts she controlled. The allegations have been a significant driver of the current push to establish an independent Inspector General's office at the county.

## What the primary source says

This record currently relies on Tier 2 reporting (Fox 13 Seattle, with corroboration in Seattle Times coverage of related DCHS oversight matters). A Tier 1 source — the county investigation report itself, any subsequent referral to the King County Prosecutor or U.S. Attorney's Office, or a charging document — has not yet been added. The record will be upgraded to `documented` and the actor named in full when that source becomes available.

## Status

Under investigation. No criminal charges have been filed against the program manager as of the last update to this record. The matter has been publicly cited by King County Councilmembers as motivation for the Inspector General proposal.

## Why it's in the registry

This is a `reported`/`alleged` case, included because the allegations come from an internal county investigation and have been reported by named broadcast journalists, and because the matter has materially shaped public reform debate. The actor is intentionally not named here pending a Tier 1 public document; the registry's purpose is structural reform, not individual exposure.

## Reform implication

Two reforms map directly to this case. First, mandatory conflict-of-interest disclosure for staff with grant-award authority, with automated cross-checks against subcontractor principals — a control the county does not currently run at scale. Second, an independent Inspector General with subpoena authority and a separate reporting line, so that investigations of grant-program staff are not housed in the same chain of command that approved their hiring and their program budget. See [reform: independent_inspector_general] and [reform: conflict_disclosure_grants].

## What happened

According to reporting first published by The Stranger and South Seattle Emerald (January 23, 2025), Pedro Gomez, Director of External Affairs in Mayor Bruce Harrell's office, was accused of rape in the second degree by Cheryl Delostrinos following a June 18, 2024 encounter that began as a business meeting Harrell had helped arrange. A police incident report describes Delostrinos waking up at a Lake Union apartment to find Gomez performing a nonconsensual sex act on her.

The Seattle Police Department investigated and referred the case to the King County Prosecuting Attorney's Office Special Assault Unit on or before January 6, 2025. Gomez resigned January 6, 2025.

## What the primary source says

This case relies on Tier 2 reporting; the underlying police incident report and prosecutor's review remain non-public as of this record. Per the Mayor's Office spokesperson statement to The Stranger: the City was notified of the investigation on September 24, 2024, "promptly placed Gomez on paid administrative leave pending the results of the investigation," and barred him from contacting City employees, engaging with community partners, or entering municipal offices while the investigation was active. The Mayor's Office stated Gomez had no prior reported allegations of sexual assault.

PubliCola subsequently reported (March 21, 2025) that additional women came forward describing uncomfortable encounters with Gomez, including a coworker who said she had not reported her own alleged assault.

## Status

Gomez resigned January 6, 2025. The King County Prosecuting Attorney's Office Special Assault Unit is reviewing the allegations. No charges have been publicly filed as of this record's last update.

## Why it's in the registry

This is a Tier 2 documented allegation against a senior mayoral-office staffer that resulted in resignation and prosecutorial referral. The allegation itself is not yet adjudicated and is recorded as "alleged" pending charging decision or other Tier 1 outcome. The case is included primarily for the structural-process question it surfaces: the ~3.5-month paid administrative leave window and the absence of public-record protocols for executive-office personnel pending criminal referral.

Per the registry's naming policy, Gomez is named because the allegation has been publicly reported in multiple Tier 2 outlets including South Seattle Emerald, PubliCola, The Stranger, and The Urbanist.

## Reform implication

This case shares the structural concern surfaced in the Diaz misconduct case (SEA-2024-001): the absence of defined timelines and Council notification requirements for paid administrative leave of senior executive-office personnel pending investigation. See [reform: personnel_oversight] and [reform: executive_accountability].

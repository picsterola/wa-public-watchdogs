## What happened

Alex Thornewell, a former Garfield High School varsity swim team member with autism, received a $125,000 settlement from Seattle Public Schools in May 2024 to resolve a civil suit (King County Superior Court Case No. 22-2-10907-6 SEA) alleging First Amendment retaliation after the school's administration silenced him for attempting to speak with a school newspaper reporter about swim team hazing.

Thornewell first witnessed hazing in 2017 as a freshman: per his account, upperclassmen would squat naked over the faces of freshmen during team activities, and he was told that "whatever occurs in the locker room remains in the locker room." During his junior year, in 2020, Thornewell agreed to speak with a reporter from the school newspaper about his experience. Before a follow-up interview could take place, the school's vice principal allegedly confronted him, pulled him into a room, and demanded he stop speaking with the reporter.

When the district opened a formal hazing investigation in the 2019-2020 school year — initiated by Garfield Athletic Director Carole Lynch, as confirmed in subsequent Public Records Act litigation — the investigator allegedly asked witnesses whether Thornewell's autism caused him to "exaggerate or fabricate details." His family characterized this line of questioning as an attempt to discredit him rather than investigate the underlying hazing.

## What the primary source says

The civil case (AT v. Seattle School District No. 1, Case No. 22-2-10907-6 SEA) was filed in 2022 and settled by May 9, 2024 for $125,000. SPS did not admit wrongdoing. A parallel Public Records Act case brought by Thornewell's mother — Amanda Thornewell v. Seattle School District No. 1, Washington Court of Appeals Case No. 85998-6-I, decided November 25, 2024 — documents the OSCR investigation timeline and identifies district personnel involved, though the Court of Appeals affirmed that the district had not violated the Public Records Act. Plaintiff attorneys were Jeff Admon (Admon Law Firm) and Lara Hruska and Luke Hackenberg (Cedar Law).

## Status

The civil settlement is final. The parallel PRA case was decided in the district's favor on the records question in November 2024; no further appeals reported in the record. The vice principal who allegedly confronted Thornewell is referenced in court documents but has not been publicly named in available reporting.

## Why it's in the registry

Tier 1 documented settlement of a First Amendment retaliation claim that the district did not contest to judgment. The case sits at the intersection of student-press freedom, hazing oversight, and disability-discrimination in the district's own investigative process. Same campus, same period as SPS-2024-001 (Garfield sex-abuse $16M settlement); both reflect Garfield athletic-program oversight failures.

## Reform implication

Three reforms map to this case. First, explicit First Amendment protections for student communications with school newspaper journalists, with administrative interference treated as actionable rather than routine. Second, hazing-response protocols that route investigations outside the building chain of command when the alleged conduct occurred at that building. Third, disability-inclusive investigation standards that prohibit using a witness's diagnosis as a basis for credibility attacks absent specific evaluative authority. See [reform: first_amendment_protections_for_students], [reform: hazing_response_protocols], [reform: disability_inclusive_investigations].

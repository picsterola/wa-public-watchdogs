**Two reports from Washington's Office of the Corrections Ombuds (OCO), published June and September 2024, found more than 3,000 currently incarcerated people had spent prolonged periods in solitary confinement between 2014 and 2023. Over ten years, 176 people self-harmed, attempted suicide, or died by suicide in solitary; 14 died by suicide. DOC pledged in 2023 to cut solitary 90% by 2028, but says it got about an eighth of the funding it asked for.**

## What happened

The Legislature directed the OCO, through a proviso in the 2023-25 operating budget (ESSB 5187), to review everyone held in solitary confinement in state prisons. The OCO's Solitary Confinement Research Team produced a two-part series ([Part I](https://oco.wa.gov/sites/default/files/OCO_SolitaryConfinementReport_Part1_June2024.pdf); [Part II](https://oco.wa.gov/sites/default/files/Solitary%20Confinement%20Part%20II_0.pdf)).

**Part I (June 2024)** built a dataset from DOC records going back decades:

- More than 3,000 currently incarcerated people had served more than 120 days in solitary in total, or more than 45 consecutive days ([Washington State Standard via Auburn Reporter](https://auburn-reporter.com/2024/08/19/report-prisoners-still-routinely-isolated-in-wa/)).
- DOC runs isolation under several unit types (Intensive Management Units, Administrative Segregation, Close Observation Areas), totaling more than 1,000 beds ([Prison Legal News](https://www.prisonlegalnews.org/news/2025/mar/1/eye-opening-self-harm-found-washington-doc-solitary-confinement/)).
- Refusing a housing assignment was the most common reason for a first placement (36%); violence accounted for 4% of first placements ([Auburn Reporter](https://auburn-reporter.com/2024/08/19/report-prisoners-still-routinely-isolated-in-wa/)).
- The OCO found DOC's own data incomplete and sometimes inconsistent ([Part I](https://oco.wa.gov/sites/default/files/OCO_SolitaryConfinementReport_Part1_June2024.pdf)).

**Part II (September 2024)** looked at conditions and outcomes. It reports that Part I "identified a total of 176 individuals who engaged in acts of self-harm, attempted suicide, or died by suicide in WADOC solitary confinement over the past 10 years. Fourteen individuals identified in the dataset died by suicide" ([Part II](https://oco.wa.gov/sites/default/files/Solitary%20Confinement%20Part%20II_0.pdf)). It also draws on interviews with 13 incarcerated people.

## About the "467 per 100,000" figure

Prison Legal News described the 14 suicides as "a rate of almost 467 per 100,000—dwarfing the United States average of 14.2" ([Prison Legal News](https://www.prisonlegalnews.org/news/2025/mar/1/eye-opening-self-harm-found-washington-doc-solitary-confinement/)). An earlier version of this record repeated that as "33 times the national average" and attributed it to the OCO. That was wrong on two counts. The OCO reports do not make that comparison. And the math does not hold: 467 per 100,000 is 14 deaths over ten years divided by a population count of about 3,000, while the 14.2 figure is a one-year national rate. The two numbers cannot be compared. See the [corrections log](/corrections).

## The 90% pledge and funding

Then-Secretary Cheryl Strange announced in January 2023 that DOC aimed to cut solitary confinement 90% by 2028. Deputy Secretary Sean Murphy later said that goal may not be reached because the agency received "about an eighth of the funding that we requested" ([Prison Legal News](https://www.prisonlegalnews.org/news/2025/mar/1/eye-opening-self-harm-found-washington-doc-solitary-confinement/)). That fraction is DOC's own characterization against its own request. DOC also told the Washington State Standard it had cut solitary by about 50% from its 2008 peak, that 602 people were in "solitary confinement conditions" as of July 1, 2024, and that it disagrees with the OCO's definition of solitary confinement ([Auburn Reporter](https://auburn-reporter.com/2024/08/19/report-prisoners-still-routinely-isolated-in-wa/)).

## Status

Updated October 20, 2026. No statute setting a solitary-confinement reduction target or deadline has been identified. The OCO has had acting leadership since its director, appointed in late 2025 after these reports were published, was removed in April 2026 (see [WA-2026-OCO-BOURGEOIS-FIRING](/cases/oco-bourgeois-firing)). An earlier version of this record said the HB 1090 statute created a 90% reduction mandate, and that the removed director had led the office during these reports. Both statements were wrong.

## Why it's in the registry

It is the most complete public accounting of solitary confinement in Washington prisons, produced at the Legislature's request, alongside an agency reduction pledge with no statutory backing and, by the agency's account, a fraction of the funding it asked for.

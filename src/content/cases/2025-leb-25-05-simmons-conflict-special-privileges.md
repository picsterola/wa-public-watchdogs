**The Washington State Legislative Ethics Board (LEB) found that Rep. Tarra Simmons (D-Bremerton) violated state ethics law three times: by sponsoring a 2024 budget proviso that funded her employer, by intervening so that employer kept receiving the money, and by trying to get the complainant disciplined. It imposed the maximum penalty on each count. Simmons denies wrongdoing and has gone to court.**

## What happened

A complaint filed February 22, 2025 (Complaint 2025 No. 5) alleged Simmons violated the Ethics in Public Service Act. The Board found reasonable cause on October 15, 2025, and held a public adjudicative hearing June 8–9, 2026, with an administrative law judge presiding over procedure ([LEB final order](https://leg.wa.gov/media/jdzoy2qi/ethicscom202505.pdf); [TVW](https://tvw.org/video/legislative-ethics-board-2026061034/)).

According to the Board's findings, Simmons worked for the Equity in Education Center (EEC) at a salary of $120,000 a year. In 2024 she sponsored a $1,000,000 budget proviso, routed through the Administrative Office of the Courts (AOC), to be split between the American Equity and Justice Group (AEJG) and a partner organization; EEC became AEJG's subcontractor. When AEJG and EEC later disputed EEC's work, the Board found Simmons called the AOC administrator handling the contract, and that the administrator then directed the parties to amend their subcontract so EEC could build its own dashboard, contrary to the proviso's plain language. The Board found Simmons' testimony that she merely read him the proviso language "not credible" ([LEB final order](https://leg.wa.gov/media/jdzoy2qi/ethicscom202505.pdf)).

## What the primary source says

The Board's August 28, 2026 final order found, by a preponderance of the evidence ([LEB final order](https://leg.wa.gov/media/jdzoy2qi/ethicscom202505.pdf)):

1. **Conflict of interest (RCW 42.52.020).** Sponsoring the 2024 proviso while employed by EEC, which received funding under it. The Board held this applies "regardless of whether Respondent was paid with the specific funds EEC received."
2. **Special privileges (RCW 42.52.070).** Using her position to intervene in the AEJG–EEC dispute so EEC kept receiving proviso funding.
3. **Special privileges (RCW 42.52.070).** Asking AEJG's leader to act against the complainant while the complaint was pending, referencing legislative action she had taken at his request.

The Board found **no violation** on the remaining allegations. It said campaign-surplus donations to AEJG fall under Public Disclosure Commission (PDC) jurisdiction, not the LEB's, though it was "troubled" by that conduct. It also found no violation arising from EEC's failure to deliver the work product.

**Penalty.** $5,000 per violation, the statutory maximum under RCW 42.52.480, for $15,000 total, plus $37,457.98 in Board staff costs, payable within 90 days of the final order. The Board found no mitigating circumstances and cited a prior violation (In re Simmons, 23-05, a $1,000 honorarium) as a factor.

## The respondent's position

Simmons denies wrongdoing. She called the penalty unfair and excessive given that three allegations were dismissed, and said she would seek review in Thurston County Superior Court, where she could be heard by "a non-political entity" and raise "repeated violations of her due process rights" ([KUOW / Washington State Standard](https://www.kuow.org/politics/2026-08-17/washington-state-lawmaker-hit-with-15k-fine-in-ethics-case)). Before the decision, her campaign said she had declined an offer to settle with a small fine, asked for the public hearing, and that "no one showed, or even claimed, that I made a single dollar for myself" ([Simmons campaign](https://www.electtarrasimmons.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Setting-the-Record-Straight.pdf)).

## Status

Updated October 20, 2026. The Washington Observer reports Simmons filed a petition for judicial review in Thurston County Superior Court dated September 25, 2026, arguing that most of the conduct was not unethical, that she was denied due process, and that the Board exceeded its authority ([Washington Observer](https://washingtonobserver.substack.com/p/wea-political-spending-tops-5-million)). The registry has not independently pulled the court docket. The LEB findings stand unless a court modifies them.

A separate complaint (26-01) about Simmons asking a primary challenger not to run was dismissed for lack of reasonable cause; the Board found she acted as a candidate, not a legislator ([LEB 26-01](https://leg.wa.gov/about-the-legislature/ethics/ethics-complaint-opinions/26-01/)). An unrelated earlier opinion, 25-38, dismissed a complaint about her Facebook post for lack of jurisdiction ([LEB 25-38](/cases/leb-25-38-simmons-special-privileges)).

## Why it's in the registry

It is an adjudicated ethics violation by a sitting legislator, decided after a rare public hearing, with the maximum penalty on every sustained count.

## Reform implication

Three maximum penalties still came to less than half the state's cost of prosecuting the case, which says more about the 1994 penalty cap than about this Board. The jurisdiction gap between the LEB and the PDC also shows up directly in the order. See [reform: ethics_enforcement_teeth] and the [LEB enforcement meta-case](/cases/leb-enforcement-pattern-meta).
